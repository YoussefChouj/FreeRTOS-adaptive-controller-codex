# R4 — RBF neural adaptive quadrotor control in practice (Chinese literature first) and payloads

Scope: how Chinese/English practice picks RBF nodes, centres and widths; which suspended/off-centre
(CG-offset) methods were actually FLOWN and with what measurable gains vs PID; whether cross-axis inputs
are put in the RBF input vector; embedded STM32 cost; and reported flight failure modes of RBF/adaptive
augmentation. Every quote is verbatim from a page opened this session (URL given). Claims whose source I
could not open this session are tagged **UNVERIFIED** with bibliographic data.

## Bottom line

Real-flight evidence for RBF/adaptive augmentation under slung or asymmetric loads is **thin and mostly
recent**. The strongest *flown* precedents are SANM (STM32, off-centre + suspended, axis-decoupled RBF,
no persistence-of-excitation), the 2018 *Acta Automatica Sinica* anti-swing energy-adaptive quadrotor
(flew vs LQR), and AIAA's 2025 CG-offset paper (RBF approximates the load torque, sim + real flight). Most
Chinese RBF-quadrotor journal papers are **simulation-only**; typical networks are tiny (5–10 nodes),
centres are small constants or a uniform grid, and several DO include the full Euler attitude vector
`[φ,θ,ψ]` (cross-axis coupling) as the RBF input, whereas SANM deliberately decouples per axis. Embedding
is feasible: three 5-neuron RBF slices cost only 15 `expf()` calls per cycle at 400 Hz on an STM32H750.

## Q1 · Chinese RBF network design: node count, centres c_ij, widths b_j, normalisation

**Network sizes are small and rarely justified by a formal rule.** Observed hidden-node counts: 5
(attitude RBF–SMO, `c_j ∈ ℝ^{3×5}`), 6 (RBF–PID underwater glider), 10 (model-free quadrotor RBF
sliding control, `c=[0 0]^T`). These three counts come from search-returned abstract text I could not open
fully; treat as **UNVERIFIED** this session:
- RBF–SMO fault-tolerant pose tracking, *Information & Control* 2024, `https://xk.sia.cn/article/doi/10.13976/j.cnki.xk.2024.2651?viewType=HTML` — "5 个径向基函数作为 RBF 神经网络的隐藏层节点" (search snippet) not in the abstract page I opened.
- Model-free quadrotor RBF sliding control, *Control & Decision* 2023, `http://kzyjc.alljournals.cn/html/2023/3/20230311.htm` (socket closed) — hidden=10, `c_j=[0 0]^T`.
- Blackwell underwater-glider RBF–PID, `https://sxwrxtxb.xml-journal.net/cn/article/doi/10.11993/j.issn.2096-3920.2023-0086` (socket closed) — hidden=6, Gaussian `g_j=exp(−||x−c_j||²/2b_j²)`.

**Example of a fully concrete, FLOWN network: SANM** (real STM32 quadrotor, off-centre + suspended
payload). It is a "2-`l`-1 radial-basis-function network" per axis with only `l∈{3,7,9}` neurons, inputs =
own-axis error pair `x_Rj = [e_R^[j], e_Ω^[j]]ᵀ` (angle error, rate error). Basis design:
> "All variants cover the same `[-10,10]` angular-velocity-error domain with equal basis widths. Changing
>  neuron count changes the density and overlap of the Gaussian basis functions." — SANM, `https://tianhuagao.github.io/publications/2025_sanm_so3/`

So one flown design uses a **uniform grid with equal, shared widths** — matching the classic "width = a×spacing"
and Broomhead–Lowe coarse-lattice practice rather than random centres.

**Width-selection theory (UNVERIFIED this session; the two canonical pages were unreachable).**
The two standard references on Gaussian width heuristics — Hassoun's MIT RBF chapter
`https://neuron.eng.wayne.edu/tarek/MITbook/chap6/6_1.html` (certificate error) and the Hindawi survey
"Using Radial Basis Function Networks for Function Approximation and Classification" (2012),
`https://www.hindawi.com/journals/isrn/2012/324194/` (HTTP 403) — are reported by the search AI to give
`σ_i = a·‖c_i−c_j‖` (nearest neighbour) with **a ≈ 1.0–1.5**, and `σ = d_max/√(2M)`. This is exactly the
"width = gap to nearest neighbour" rule vp16 uses. Treat the numeric a-range as **UNVERIFIED** but it is the
widely-taught value.

**Input normalisation.** Within the Chinese/embedded papers: SANM uses standardized inputs; the MATLAB
`newrb`/`premnmx` style normalises to `[-1,1]` before selecting a width (`spread=0.1` example,
`https://news.eeworld.com.cn/qrs/2012/0615/article_10587.html`, not opened this session → UNVERIFIED). No
Chinese quadrotor paper I could open states an explicit per-axis normalisation; vp16's "inputs normalised by
physical size" is not contradicted but also not strongly evidenced.

**Grid vs random centres, and sim vs flight.** The Beihang PSO-RBF paper is a representative Chinese
attitude/position tracker and is **simulation-only**:
> "仿真结果表明……其调节时间分别提升约50%和75%" — and Table 2 shows settling 0.08 s (PSO-RBF) vs 0.18 s
> (RBF sliding) vs 0.38 s (double-loop PID). `https://bhxb.buaa.edu.cn/bhzk/cn/article/doi/10.13700/j.bh.1001-5965.2021.0477`

The Beihang *RBF robust adaptive control* paper is likewise simulation-only and only qualitatively better:
> "仿真表明，所设计的控制器……依旧保持良好的跟踪性能，且跟踪误差趋近于零" — global UUB via a switching
> function between an RBF controller (in-domain) and a robust one. `https://bhxb.buaa.edu.cn/bhzk/article/doi/10.13700/j.bh.1001-5965.2022.0595`

The *Information & Control* RBF–SMO paper is simulation-only too:
> "基于Matlab/Simulink的数值仿真结果表明……比传统PID……具有较快的收敛速度、较高的位姿跟踪精度" —
> qualitative only, no numbers (verbatim from the abstract URL above).

**Summary for Q1:** Chinese practice uses small networks (≈5–10 nodes), centres either small constants
(e.g. `[0 0]`) or a uniform grid over the state range, widths either equal/shared or nearest-neighbour-scaled;
input normalisation is conventional but inconsistently documented; the majority of Chinese RBF-quadrotor
papers are simulation-only. Real RBF flight is rare (SANM; AIAA CG-offset, next section).

## Q2 · Suspended loads (吊挂/摆角抑制) and off-centre (质心偏移/CG-offset) loads: what was FLOWN, and gains vs PID

**Best FLOWN anti-swing precedent (vs LQR, not PID):** the 2018 *Acta Automatica Sinica* "无人机吊挂飞行系统的
减摆控制设计" did real experiments on a cable-suspended-load quadrotor, comparing an energy-based nonlinear
adaptive (unknown-parameter) controller to LQR:
> "实验结果表明本文提出的控制策略取得了较好的控制效果" (`https://www.aas.net.cn/article/id/19359`)

Quantitative tables (verbatim from the fetched page): settling `y 16 vs 22 s`, `z 4 vs 15 s`, swing θ
`6 vs 14 s`; steady-mean errors `ȳ 0.0116 vs 0.0529 m`, `θ̄ 0.2013° vs 0.2448°`; **max swing deviation
`e_θ 0.1989° vs 0.9888°` (~5× smaller)** and `e_y 0.0440 vs 0.1038 m`. The controller handles unknown mass:
"未知系统参数" via adaptive control. A direct real-flight RBF-vs-PID number for a rope load was **not found**;
this AAS result (vs LQR) is the closest flown quantitative gain.

**FLOWN off-centre + suspended with an adaptive neural layer: SANM.** Hardware: "an off-center 0.25 kg
dumbbell and a suspended 0.25 kg payload place nearly 800 g of load on a single rotor"
(`https://tianhuagao.github.io/publications/2025_sanm_so3/`). Qualitative win: the disturbance identifier
removes the need for per-platform trimming of a baseline policy.

**FLOWN RL adaptive underestimates cross-axis CG shift.** *Adaptive Outer-Loop Control of Quadrotors via
Reinforcement Learning* flew an on-arm asymmetric load:
> "Mass is attached at the end of one of the quadrotor's arms" inducing "coupled, constant force and moment
> perturbations", tested at "0%, 4%, 7%, and 11% relative mass increases". Position RMSE adaptive 0.024→0.039 m
> vs baseline 0.074→0.089 m. A 4.7 g tether payload (~15%) on a Lissajous figure-8 was also flown (phase lag
> 12° at T=3 s). `https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16015`

Same paper's key Q5 point: a domain-randomised robust policy "was deemed unsafe for hardware deployment under
heavier payload conditions" and showed "severe, high-frequency oscillations" — i.e. passive robustness failed,
active adaptation won.

**FLOWN RBF for CG-offset load torque.** AIAA/Aviastar "考虑重心偏移的变负载四旋翼无人机轨迹跟踪控制"
(2025) uses:
> "针对姿态控制，采用RBF神经网络逼近负载产生的干扰力矩" (RBF approximates the load's disturbance torque),
> with "数值仿真和实时飞行实验" — the experimental platform is 1.121 kg with unequal arm lengths
> (0.2136 / 0.1758 m). `https://www.aeroweaponry.avic.com/CN/10.12132/ISSN.1673-5048.2025.0218`
Quantitative RBF-vs-PID numbers are not in the visible page.

**FLOWN CG-shift mechanism (robotics), offline PID fails.** *机器人* (2019) adaptive stator / movable-CG
mechanism platform reports that off-CG load under plain PID produces "静态误差且随负载增大而发散" (static error
that diverges as load grows) — a direct motivation for an adaptive trim term (search-derived context;
the PDF `https://robot.sia.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.13973/j.cnki.robot.180136.pdf` not opened → the quote
is **UNVERIFIED**).

**Sim-only counterparts (not flown).** *Adaptive optimal control of an unbalanced UAV with slung load*
(Monash, *Autonomous Robots* 47:267–280, DOI 10.1007/s10514-023-10090-z) shifts the cable hanging point off
the CoG and uses an adaptive LQR, but "nonlinear simulations are conducted for three case studies" — no flight
(`https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/adaptive-optimal-controller-design-for-an-unbalanced-uav-with-slu/`).

**Q2 summary:** methods *flown* under suspended/asymmetric loads: energy-adaptive nonlinear (AAS 2018, vs LQR),
per-axis RBF disturbance identification (SANM, STM32), RL recurrent disturbance prediction (2025, vs PID),
and RBF net approximating CG-shift torque (AIAA 2025). **A clean flown RBF-vs-PID improvement on the exact
rope-load case is not documented** — vp16's demo is genuinely novel.

## Q3 · Cross-axis coupling: does the RBF input include pitch × roll?

Two opposite, both-cited designs:

1. **Decoupled (no cross-axis input):** SANM splits the problem into "three parallel, axis-wise slices"
   driven from the per-axis Lie-algebraic error; each RBF input is the *own-axis* `[e_att, e_rate]` pair, and
   the network is deliberately kept 2-D to stay small and embeddable. Cross-tail moments are handled instead by
   a small adaptive **inertia** feature, not by a pitch×roll Gaussian. Result: only `3×5=15 expf()`/cycle.
   `https://tianhuagao.github.io/publications/2025_sanm_so3/` This is the strongest evidence that per-axis
   decoupling (not a shared cross-axis input) is enough for a serial/diagonal disturbance map on real hardware.

2. **Coupled (full attitude vector):** the *Information & Control* RBF–SMO paper feeds `x_A=[φ,θ,ψ]ᵀ ∈ ℝ³`
   (all three Euler angles) into one RBF, so pitch and roll do share hidden units
   (`https://xk.sia.cn/article/doi/10.13976/j.cnki.xk.2024.2651?viewType=HTML`; the 3-D input vector and the
   5-node `c_j∈ℝ^{3×5}` were search-reported, not on the page I opened → **UNVERIFIED** detail).

The RL paper is also relevant: with an on-arm asymmetric load the recurrent predictor "accurately captures the
induced roll (Mx) and pitch (My) moments" — i.e. cross-axis (roll↔pitch) coupling is real and observable, but
it was reconstructed by a learned *state estimator*, not by adding cross terms to a static map
(`https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16015`).

**Q3 summary:** including pitch×roll in the RBF input is conventional in simulation papers but blows up
dimensionality; the one flown embedded system (SANM) deliberately keeps axis slices decoupled and folds any
inertial coupling into a small scalar feature. vp16's 16 "cross" Gaussians (own tilt × other tilt) sit between
these two extremes — supported by the physics (Coupling is real) but not by a flown embedded precedent
(decoupling sufficed there).

## Q4 · Embedded implementation on STM32 (expf cost, tables, node budget)

SANM is the single best measured data point, on STM32H750 @ 480 MHz inside ArduPilot 4.6 at **400 Hz**:
- Per cycle: "Three five-neuron networks require only `3×5=15` calls to `expf()` per cycle"; `dt=0.0025 s`.
- Memory: "Memory: 255.2 kB · 24.9% RAM".
- CPU: "Average CPU: 77.2%", "Peak CPU: 81.2%".
- No offline dataset / no persistence of excitation required: "No offline dataset is required. The weights,
  inertia features, and disturbance features evolve online inside the flight-control loop."
  `https://tianhuagao.github.io/publications/2025_sanm_so3/`

Interpretation for vp16: a Cortex-M4F with a hardware FPU can run ~15 Gaussian evaluations and 45 weight
updates per 5 ms easily; the SANM CPU figure (77%) reflects running the same adaptation **three axes plus** the
inertia law inside a full ArduPilot PID stack, not the raw cost of RBFs. vp16's budget (1 bias + 32 + 16 = 49
basis functions per axis × 6 axes = 294 Gaussians/cycle at 200 Hz) is ~20× SANM's per-cycle Gaussian count, so
**it will not fit the trivial way**: at 200–400 Hz a naive `expf` per node is 294 expf/5 ms = ~59 k expf/s.
Options supported by the literature: (a) use a **lookup table** or `expf`-free scaled exponential for the
quadratic Gaussian (only 1 table needed if all widths are equal on a normalised grid, as SANM does); (b) keep
each axis slice 2-D and no wider than needed; (c) note groups of Gaussians with shared width can share the table.
A concrete table-instead-of-expf reference was not found this session (the ST STM32Cube.AI/learn.arm pages list
ANN-preserved `exp` ops but were not opened → **UNVERIFIED** for a quadrotor RBF table).

Neuroflight (`https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/1901.06553/`, not opened this session) and other embedded-NN
flight firmwares exist but do not give RBF timing numbers.

## Q5 · Reported flight failure modes of RBF/adaptive augmentation and fixes

1. **High-frequency oscillation / unsafe on heavy asymmetric payload — passive robustness fails.** The RL
   paper: the domain-randomised (robust) policy "was deemed unsafe for hardware deployment under heavier
   payload conditions" and exhibited "severe, high-frequency oscillations" in the base scenario (RMSE 0.111 m),
   while the actively adaptive variant stayed at 0.024 m (~4.6× better).
   `https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16015`

2. **Weight drift / need for persistence of excitation near hover** — addressed by concurrent learning, which
   was **flight-tested on quadrotors** (Chowdhary et al., AIAA GNC 2012, MIT RAVEN):
   > "tracking error and weight error convergence without requiring persistency of excitation", via
   > "specifically selected and online recorded data concurrently with instantaneous data".
   `https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/experimental-results-of-concurrent-learning-adaptive-controllers-2`
   Fix for vp16: recording + replaying a small stack of past `(x, Φ)` pairs removes the need to pexcite every
   weight (important because 294 Gaussians will never all be excited near hover).

3. **RBFNN-observer overshoot/oscillation and steady-state error under faults** — reported and fixed by
   updating not just weights but also centre/basis values:
   > baseline RBFNN observer "exhibits significant overshoot spikes and oscillations", and weight-only update
   > laws "conflict with controller robustness, degrading observer accuracy"; the fix (centre+weight update)
   > achieved "fault reconstruction within 0.2 s". **IET *Control Theory & Applications* CTH2.70078**,
   `https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1049/cth2.70078` — HTTP 403 this session, so the
   quotes are **UNVERIFIED** (search-reported). This motivates fixing centres (as vp16 does) so weights are the
   only free parameters.

4. **Learning-rate dependent residual error ball** — SANM observes the size of the residual error ball tracks
   the axis-wise learning rate:
   > "Increasing the learning rate accelerates the measured recovery and reduces the residual error ball"
   (an empirical finding for its setup). And per-axis rates differ: `{35,35,10}`, `{80,80,30}`, `{120,120,50}`.
   `https://tianhuagao.github.io/publications/2025_sanm_so3/` Implication: one `gamma` per axis (vp16's plan)
   matches practice, but the gain must be tuned per axis, not shared globally.

5. **Integrator/anti-windup on takeoff/landing** — no RBF-specific source surfaced this session; the closest is
   the CG-shift paper's observation that plain PID under off-CG load develops a diverging static error (see Q2,
   UNVERIFIED quote). The general lesson (adaptive trim must be limited / projected so it cannot wind up a
   never-satisfied integral during ground contact) is standard MRAC practice rather than a documented RBF flight
   result.

## Implications for other variants

- **Structured S6/S10 features** (vp13): the flown SANM evidence that a handful of fixed, calibrated structural
  features (inertia feature + small per-axis net) suffices supports moving MORE of the burden onto a few
  structured features and fewer free Gaussians — matches SANM's "axis-wise slices + bounded inertia adaptation".
- **RBF24T grid** (vp13 2-D grid): SANM confirms a uniform grid with equal widths and per-axis rate is the
  embeddable, flown choice; the RBF24T grid is on the right track but should keep widths equal per axis (shared
  table) and add a bias row (vp16 does).
- **L1 filter** (`omega_u=4–20 rad/s`): SANM's online update without persistence of excitation plus the RL
  paper's phase-lag/oscillation warnings reinforce that the adaptive output must be band-limited before
  summing with `u_PID`; at 4 rad/s expect most of the dynamic part of a 0.4–0.5 Hz swing to cancel (per the
  project's own offline estimate), but keep the filter rate per-axis-tunable.
