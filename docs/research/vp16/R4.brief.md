## Our system (context; do not research this part)
- Quadrotor 988 g plus payload: a 570 g bottle on a 33 cm rope (measured swing peak 0.39-0.49 Hz), or a 293 g rigid
  arm load mounted off-centre (asymmetric). STM32F4 (Cortex-M4F, 168 MHz), FreeRTOS, attitude stabilizer at 200 Hz.
- Baseline: cascaded PID. Angle -> rate -> torque; position -> velocity -> accel (cm/s^2) -> lean angle, 15 deg clamp.
- Adaptive layer = MRAC augmentation on top of the PID: u = u_PID + u_ad, u_ad = LPF(omega_u = 4-20 rad/s) of
  -Theta^T Phi(x). Error e = x - x_m (x = body rate, x_m from a first-order reference model). Law:
  s = e_sat*tanh(e/e_sat); Theta_dot = gamma*( -s*Phi/(1+||Phi||^2) - sigma*Theta ), sigma = 0.01, plus projection
  to per-weight limits. Code: API/mrac.c (you may read it).
- Measured (variant vp13: 24 Gaussians on rate x tilt plus 6 structured features): equal to PID on the 293 g arm
  load, no win. u_ad carries 45-60% of the static trim (the PID integrator carries the rest). In the swing band
  0.25-0.9 Hz the pitch u_ad ADDS disturbance (phase -70..-101 deg against the ideal). Offline: an ideal estimator of
  the unmodelled torque behind a first-order filter at 4 rad/s would cancel ~40-60% of its dynamic part, at 16 rad/s
  ~90%.
- Next variant vp16 (designed, not built): per axis (pitch, roll, yaw, Z, and NEW X/Y on the velocity loops at
  100 Hz) = bias row (phi = 1, limit 15% of authority) + 32 "own" Gaussians (2-D grid: rate x angle error, centres
  +-0.05/0.15/0.3/0.6 x +-0.2/0.6 in normalised units, dense near hover) + 16 "cross" Gaussians (own tilt x other
  tilt, etc.). Each bump is limited to 1% of authority. Width sigma = gap to the nearest neighbouring centre. One
  gamma per axis: gamma = lim*(1+||Phi||^2)/(e_sat*T), T = 3 s fill time. Inputs normalised by physical size. All 6
  axes inject on the first flight. Goal: a stable demo that clearly beats PID with the 570 g rope load, then the arm.
