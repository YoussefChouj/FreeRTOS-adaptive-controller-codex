- **Does routing beat unrouted at equal effort?** No (H6 KILLED). All three routed modes are worse on tune
  (CI > 0) and on test.
- **Does predictive help beyond reactive?** Not detectably (H6b KILLED): both - reactive = -0.0033
  [-0.0094, +0.0005]. The point estimate and zigzag xtrack (0.041 vs 0.049) favour both. Predictive vs
  unrouted on the zigzag and step rows (135 rows, `h6b_subset.py`): +0.037 [-0.016, +0.045].

**Why routing does not help** (`yard_3l.py`, `diag_3l_pred.py`):
1. *Gain concentration.* The routed tunes chose gamma 2.2-3.0 (unrouted 0.36), w_max 1.4-2.2 (0.42) and ran
   u_ad/u_nom 0.23-0.28 (0.085). A gate that puts its weight on one or two bands multiplies the effective
   adaptation gain in those bands, and the adaptive term overreacts.
2. *Reference-model mismatch.* The reference model misses the plant by 18-27% RMSE, so the error s_err that
   the reactive gate reads is mostly model error, not the disturbance tones the bands are meant to separate.
3. *The predictive layer has nothing to act on for steps.* Its gate is driven by preview acceleration, which
   is zero on step references, so it stays uniform there. It does move on curved references (roll-band
   time-std 0.02-0.14 on zigzag_1.0 and circle_1.0), but no row set showed a gain.
4. What 3L adds over MRAC_S6 (0.073 vs 0.099) therefore comes from the 6-feature regressor basis at low gamma,
   not from routing. This is an inference: no basis-only ablation was run, and unrouted 3L differs from S6 in
   more than the basis.

**Fly pid_tuned2 first as the in-flight reference.** It is the only tag that passes every constraint.

## Sim-to-real risks
1. Plant fit is poor (NRMSE ~1). Rankings assume the sim's error structure transfers; margins of a few
   percent (e.g. rbf12 vs pid_tuned2, -4%) are inside that uncertainty.
2. Control effectiveness b: 8 was chosen from {8, 11, 16}. At b = 16 every adaptive loop gain doubles;
   adaptive gains tuned at b = 8 can limit-cycle. Flight stage 1 checks this (hover, adaptation frozen).
3. Tuned gains trade robustness: pid_tuned2 and mrac_rbf12 diverge on more than half of combo_unseen rows,
   which the tuner never saw. The flight envelope must stay inside the tuned families until proven.
4. Authority: FLIGHT8 yaw imbalance holds U 450-650 and drives M3/M4 into the 4000 clamp. The sim models
   imbalance 430; if the real one is larger, saturation dominates and adaptive laws can wind up.
5. Ground windup: FLIGHT8 showed U = 650 before liftoff. Any adaptive term must be frozen and reset until
   liftoff (the sim starts airborne, so it does not test this).
6. Estimator: the sim gives noise + bias + lag, not the real EKF/optical-flow drift modes. Controllers that
   use accelerations or regressors on rate derivatives are exposed to vibration the sim does not model.
7. Motor model: first-order lag and fixed 15 ms delay; real ESC/prop nonlinearity and battery sag
   (14.0 V in FLIGHT8) change b in flight (the battery family covers only part of this).
